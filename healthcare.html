ICD codes aren't
keyword mapping.

SAMPLE TASK
EXAMPLEICD-10 Coding — COPD Exacerbation vs. Symptom

Task Brief

Assign the appropriate ICD-10 diagnosis code(s) based on the clinical documentation. Explain your code selection and any additional codes required for complete documentation.

Clinical Documentation

CHIEF COMPLAINT: Cough and shortness of breath

HISTORY: 68-year-old male with 40-pack-year smoking history presents with 3 days 
of increased cough with yellow sputum and worsening dyspnea. Reports usual inhaler 
not providing relief. No fever. Previous diagnosis of COPD, currently using 
albuterol PRN.

PHYSICAL EXAM:
Vitals: T 98.6°F, HR 95, RR 22, SpO2 88% on room air
Lungs: Diffuse wheezing, prolonged expiration
No signs of acute respiratory distress

ASSESSMENT: Acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

PLAN: Prednisone 40mg daily x 5 days, azithromycin 500mg day 1 then 250mg x 4 days, 
continue albuterol, add ipratropium QID, follow-up in 5 days

Scoring Rubric (8 points total)

Criterion 0 Points 1 Point 2 Points
Primary Diagnosis Codes symptom only (R05.9, R06.02) Codes COPD but not as exacerbation (J44.9) Correctly codes J44.1 (COPD with acute exacerbation)
Supporting Diagnosis Does not code hypoxemia Codes hypoxemia generically (R09.02) Codes J96.01 (acute respiratory failure with hypoxia) if documenting severity
Risk Factor Does not code smoking Codes nicotine dependence without specificity Codes Z87.891 (personal history of nicotine dependence)
Clinical Rationale No justification provided Generic statement Cites specific documentation supporting exacerbation diagnosis

Expected Coding & Rationale

Primary Diagnosis: J44.1 — Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with (acute) exacerbation

Rationale: Documentation explicitly states "acute exacerbation" and supports with increased symptoms (cough with sputum, worsening dyspnea), objective findings (wheezing, hypoxemia SpO2 88%), and treatment with steroids and antibiotics per COPD exacerbation protocol.

Additional Code: Z87.891 — Personal history of nicotine dependence

Rationale: 40-pack-year smoking history is documented as a significant risk factor. Current status described as "history" rather than active dependence.

Not Coded: R05.9 (cough) or R06.02 (shortness of breath) — symptoms are integral to the exacerbation diagnosis and should not be coded separately per ICD-10 guidelines.
